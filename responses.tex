\textbf{Referee 2}

\begin{referee}
    This is a very important topic that many data scientists will find interesting. I recommend publication.
I believe it would improve accessibility if more time was spent in the abstract and introduction linking
containers to the regular work of data scientist: for example, why is this article being written? what
prompted it? what does the author hope the reader will get out of it? What is the connection to
Census (this is central and never clearly explained)?

\end{referee}
\begin{response}

Thank you for framing the questions in this way to broaden the audience for the article. I have added or expanded some sentences to the introduction to reference the current alternatives that are "costly to maintain" and require more effort from the researchers.

\begin{quote}
As an alternative, data custodians provide confidential data to researchers in restricted-access environments, often still requiring physical presence in expensive to maintain secure computer rooms managed by the data custodian or universities \citep{weinbergAccessMethodsUnited2007,vilhuberProtectingConfidentialData2024}.  

This article will set out to combine various lessons learned from both the literature and past experiments in a variety of domains to propose an alternative or complementary approach to providing access to confidential data via synthetic data or physical access, reducing the effort required by researchers to access these data. 
\end{quote}

The connection to the Census is not central at all to the arguments here. It is used as a motivating example, because of the author's prior experience with the SSB on the Synthetic Data Server. But the mechanism is a general one. The Census Bureau is, of course, central to the illustrative use case (starting on page 11). To avoid the impression that this is primarily about the Census Bureau, I chosen to  de-emphasize the Census Bureau (and Codeocean) in the example:

    \begin{quote}
Without loss of generality, the use case is specifically chosen to extend the type of analysis previously conducted on the \ac{SDS} with \ac{SSB} data, relying on validation by the U.S. Census Bureau. 
    \end{quote}

    and in some of the subsequent sentences:

    \begin{quote}
        In this configuration, the data owner (here: Census Bureau) could allow the container system provider (here: Codeocean) to house both the synthetic dataset as well as...
        \end{quote}
\end{response}

\begin{referee}
A question that many readers will have pertains to the recommendation to use commercial vendors,
ie. Code Ocean. With vendors there is always the risk they vanish, or change policies, etc. How does
the author propose the research community mitigates this risk, while still enabling access to and
validation of confidential data? Readers will remember when this exact thing happened to docker /
docker hub a few years ago which was widely used as a free service for container hosting, like Code
Ocean today, then became pay to play with a policy change.
\end{referee}

\begin{response}
I had partially addressed the fact that vendors can go away in a footnote in the text. I have moved and elevated this footnote into a full paragraph in a section on "Caveats". I note that some vendors have disappeared, but that this is fundamentally not the same problem. The use of any provider is primarily a transient process, destined to produce the final analysis in the confines of the statistical agency. It is the final output that primarily needs preservation, not the intermediate output produced via a platform. That being said, as the referee's next comment points out, the reproducible package generated with the low-quality synthetic data has value, as a more accessible replication package. I disuss this, without expanding too much. 

\end{response}
